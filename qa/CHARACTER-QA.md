# Gate 2 Checklist

## 1. Lily Has an Independent Goal

**PASS**

Lily's goal is to build an adult life that feels like her own.

This explicitly includes choosing her future rather than allowing:
- grief;
- family fear;
- Max's crisis;

to determine it for her.

She therefore retains a life direction that can conflict with the dimensional romance.

## 2. Max Has an Independent Goal

**PASS**

Max wants to:
- return to his family;
- discover what caused the overlap;
- prevent another collapse.

He must pursue those goals even when Lily disagrees with his methods.

His plot function therefore survives outside romance.

## 3. Both Main Characters Can Drive Plot

**PASS**

Lily can drive story through:
- apartment decisions;
- Earth-side investigation;
- social consequences;
- observation;
- experiments;
- choices about independence.

Max can drive story through:
- Velaire investigation;
- archive knowledge;
- spatial-cartography skills;
- family search;
- risky experiments;
- opposition to containment decisions.

Neither is structurally dependent on the other to initiate action.

## 4. Both Main Characters Have Real Flaws

**PASS**

Lily:
- refuses help;
- overplans;
- assumes the worst;
- retreats when fearing loss.

Max:
- hides fear with humor;
- takes risks alone;
- becomes secretive;
- acts before asking;
- ties worth to usefulness.

Their flaws can naturally generate conflict without villainizing either lead.

## 5. Humor Styles Differ

**PASS**

Lily:
- dry;
- practical;
- straight-faced;
- comedy through restraint and embarrassment.

Max:
- playful;
- impulsive;
- exaggerated;
- comedy through confidence, experimentation, and badly timed realization.

Their humor can interact without requiring identical banter voices.

## 6. Dialogue Voices Differ

**PASS**

The Character Bible and Writing Rules distinguish Lily and Max clearly.

Supporting dialogue identities are also defined for:
- Ava;
- June;
- Nora;
- Inez;
- Mina;
- Elara;
- Corven.

The cast is not written as one generic speaking voice.

# Additional Character Integrity Checks

## Wounds Are Distinct

**PASS**

Lily's wound centers on sudden loss and fear of attachment.

Max's wound centers on disappearance, usefulness, responsibility, and fear of being forgotten.

They are thematically compatible without being copies.

## False Beliefs Are Distinct

**PASS**

Lily:

Max:
"If I stop being useful, people will stop waiting for me."

These can create different romantic mistakes.

## Romantic Dynamic Is Reciprocal

**PASS**

Lily may ground Max's impulsiveness.

Max may challenge Lily's overcontrol.

The canon explicitly states that neither:
- fixes;
- completes;
- owns;

the other.

Both can say no.

## Supporting Agency

**PASS**

The Character Agency Rule now requires recurring characters to be capable of decisions not ordered by Lily or Max.

This is strong enough for later arc planning.

# Minor Notes

## NOTE-001 — Avoid Role Rigidity

Lily is usually the straight-faced comedy half and Max is usually the instigator.

Do not turn this into a permanent formula.

Allow:
- Lily to cause chaos;
- Max to become the practical one;
- both to fail differently.

This will prevent repetitive romantic-comedy scenes.

**Severity:** MINOR / NON-BLOCKING  
**Owner:** A048 Romantic Comedy Editor

## NOTE-002 — Lily's Active-Arc Goal Must Become Concrete

"Build an adult life that feels like hers" is strong series-level motivation but broad.

During Gate 6 Aster House Arc Architecture, Lily must receive a specific local goal with measurable pressure.

Examples of category, not locked content:
- keeping the apartment;
- maintaining work;
- proving she can live independently;
- solving one bounded apartment problem.

**Severity:** MINOR / NON-BLOCKING  
**Owner:** A061 Arc Architect + A043 Lily Guardian
