# Formal Experiment

**Question:** Can the overlap change the state/details of a larger object positioned near the correlated room coordinate?

**Setup:**
- Lily places her wobbly wooden dining chair beside the east-side desk, with its front legs aligned to taped floor marks.
- Max places his metal work stool at the corresponding Room 17 position.
- Each describes the starting object through bounded writing before the test.
- Lily's chair: wood, spindle back, one short leg with folded-cardboard shim.
- Max's stool: metal frame, square seat, no spindle back, no cardboard shim.

**Prediction:** If only information crosses, one or both objects may temporarily show details from the other side without moving worlds.

**Test:** Hold both objects at the marked position through one coherence window and compare observations.

**Actual Result:**
- Lily's chair does not become Max's stool.
- It briefly becomes a **hybrid state**: its wooden seat remains, but a dark metal cross-brace appears under it and the back geometry shifts into a shape neither starting object has as a complete object.
- Max simultaneously reports his stool showing a wooden rung/cardboard-like edge detail inconsistent with his starting stool.
- The changes fade after roughly 20–30 seconds.

**Cost:**
- Lily experiences cold fingertips and brief nausea.
- No injury.

**Learning:** object/furniture state can partially overlap; matter does not simply teleport.
