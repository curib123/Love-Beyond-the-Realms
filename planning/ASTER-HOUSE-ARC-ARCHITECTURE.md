# 1. Arc Purpose

The Aster House Arc is the opening active arc.

It must establish:
- Lily Marlowe as a complete protagonist with an Earth life;
- Max Arden as a complete protagonist with a Velaire life and independent problem;
- the cross-world roommate romantic-comedy engine;
- the first repeatable rules of the overlap;
- a trustworthy Lily/Max working relationship;
- a satisfying local resolution without solving the series mystery.

The arc must feel like a complete opening story, not a long prologue.

# 4. Lily's Concrete Arc Goal

**Keep Unit 4B as a functional home and prove to herself that she can manage independent adult life while determining whether Max is real and whether the apartment is safe enough to stay in.**

Success for this arc does not mean "solve the dimensions."

By the local resolution Lily should:
- make an informed choice to remain in Unit 4B;
- keep at least a workable relationship with work/friends/family;
- understand that Max is a real person rather than a conventional ghost;
- establish practical boundaries for sharing the overlapping space;
- choose cooperation without abandoning her independence.

# 5. Max's Concrete Arc Goal

**Prove Lily is a real living person, establish a repeatable way to perceive/communicate with her, and determine whether the overlap can become a useful path toward contacting his own world again.**

By the local resolution Max should:
- know Lily is real;
- establish a repeatable communication/testing protocol with her;
- achieve limited P1 Indirect Sight under narrow conditions;
- accept that brute-force escalation is unsafe;
- remain phase-bound and still unable to return to his family.

The arc gives Max progress and hope, not a complete rescue.

# 6. Core Romantic-Comedy Engine

Comedy comes primarily from **two incompatible lives occupying one correlated room**.

Recurring engines:
- Lily can see Max before Max can see Lily;
- Max talks to an unseen Lily and guesses wrong about what she is doing;
- both try to use the same room coordinate differently;
- Echo Exchange changes object states at bad times;
- Phase Lag ruins timing;
- Lily needs privacy while Max does not understand what she can currently see;
- Max confidently explains a bad theory;
- Earth objects confuse Max;
- Velaire objects confuse Lily;
- Lily's friends/neighbours hear one side of a conversation;
- experiments interrupt normal routines;
- sincere moments are occasionally broken by practical overlap problems.

Comedy rules:
- no joke quota;
- no humiliation cruelty;
- do not joke through grief, injury, or dangerous climax beats;
- occasionally reverse the usual pattern so Lily causes the chaos and Max becomes the practical one.

# 7. Ordinary-Life Pressure

The arc must repeatedly prove that both protagonists have lives outside the romance.

## Lily-side pressure

Use some combination of:

Dimensional events should interfere with ordinary life, not replace it.

## Max-side pressure

The arc must preserve the cost of separation from normal Velaire life through:

- evidence of people searching or responding to his disappearance;
- concern for Elara;
- unresolved uncertainty about Mina;
- Larkspur-side access or safety pressure.

The arc must not turn Max's world into an empty laboratory.

Mina's survival remains protected from Max.

# 8. Central Local Mystery

The arc should move from:

# 10. Sci-Fi Mechanic Focus

Gate 6 locks the opening arc's science focus to:

1. **P0 Bleed**
   - establish asymmetric perception.

2. **Basic Phase Lag**
   - delayed/early communication in small, measurable amounts.
   - no major causal exploitation.

3. **Basic Echo Exchange**
   - temporary state changes;
   - sound/light overlap;
   - small object/surface effects;
   - short-lived geometry errors.

4. **Anchor Observation**
   - Lily's brass key;
   - wall mirror;
   - Max's spatial compass;
   - treated as correlation tools, not magic artifacts.

5. **P1 Indirect Sight**
   - Max gains limited sight of Lily through a reflective/instrument-mediated condition.
   - P1 is the highest perception level intentionally unlocked by this arc.

End-state ceilings:
- default perception may still be P0;
- P1 becomes reproducible only under narrow conditions;
- **C0 remains unchanged**;
- **X0 remains unchanged**;
- no intentional physical crossing;
- no sustained touch.

# 11. Experiment Architecture

Experiments must escalate by question, not spectacle.

## Experiment Class A — Reality Test

Question:
- Are Lily and Max independent living people?

Possible observations:
- simultaneous descriptions;
- object placement;
- room differences;
- timed responses.

Purpose:
- move R0 toward R1.

## Experiment Class B — Timing Test

Question:
- Is communication delay random?

Possible observations:
- counting;
- taps;
- clock comparison;
- short response patterns.

Purpose:
- establish simple Phase Lag.

## Experiment Class C — State Test

Question:
- Why do some objects/surfaces change?

Possible observations:
- controlled object positions;
- marks;
- temperature;
- light.

Purpose:
- establish repeatable Echo Exchange without explaining the full physics.

## Experiment Class D — Perception Test

Question:
- Can Max see Lily indirectly?

Possible tools:
- wall mirror;
- polished surface;
- Max's compass/instrument;
- Lily's key as an observed correlation object.

Purpose:
- earn P1 at the midpoint.

Every experiment must have:
- prediction;
- result;
- cost;
- changed understanding.

# 13. Arc Movement — No Chapter Count

The architecture uses five functional movements, not fixed chapter blocks.

## Movement A — Impossible Room

Purpose:
- Lily moves in;
- Max is established from his own POV;
- each interprets the other incorrectly;
- first roommate comedy;
- R0 is clear;
- M-001 becomes ACTIVE.

End condition:
- both accept that the phenomenon reacts to the other presence.

## Movement B — Prove It

Purpose:
- mutual reality tests;
- basic timed communication;
- ordinary-life interruptions;
- first useful rules;
- R1 begins.

End condition:
- both have evidence that the other is not an imagined voice/ordinary haunting.

## Movement C — I Can See You

**Midpoint shift**

Lily and Max design a controlled perception test.

Max achieves the first limited **P1 Indirect Sight** of Lily through an authorized reflective/instrument condition.

Consequences:
- Max can finally attach a face/body to the voice;
- Lily experiences the emotional reversal of being observed after having the visual advantage;
- their banter changes because the asymmetry is no longer absolute;
- both become more confident that careful experiments can improve contact.

Relationship:
- R1 deepens toward early **R2 — Cross-World Roommates**.

This is a character/mechanic breakthrough, not a cosmology reveal.

## Movement D — Push Too Far

**Meaningful mistake / reversal**

Because the P1 test worked, Lily and Max become overconfident.

They attempt to extend or repeat the effect using multiple correlation/anchor conditions too aggressively.

The experiment causes an unstable local event such as:
- stronger Echo Exchange;
- room geometry mismatch;
- temporary wrong-door/hallway view;
- object damage;
- severe Phase Lag;
- loss of reliable communication;
- social/building consequences outside their private routine.

Critical rule:
- they may conclude **"we pushed this test too hard"**;
- they may **not** conclude MT-003 that repeated experiments are progressively strengthening the leak over time.

Character consequence:
- Lily's apartment goal is threatened by complaints/damage/safety concern;
- Max must confront that being useful does not justify reckless experimentation.

End condition:
- a local instability remains active enough that they must fix what they caused instead of continuing experimentation.

## Movement E — Stabilize, Then Choose

**Climax**

The local overlap destabilizes in a way directly caused by the failed test.

Lily and Max must use information learned earlier:
- measured lag;
- object-state behavior;
- anchor reactions;
- their communication protocol.

They do **not** discover a new power during the climax.

The solution requires:
- coordinated timing despite Phase Lag;
- deliberately reducing or separating the conditions that amplified the event;
- choosing safety over a stronger moment of connection.

Possible external pressure:
- June/Inez/Nora may be close enough that Lily must protect ordinary people from the apartment problem;
- Velaire-side safety activity may threaten Max's access to Room 17.

Climax success:
- they return the overlap to a survivable baseline;
- no stable passage opens;
- no touch occurs;
- no major twist is revealed.

# 14. Causal Climax Chain

The climax must be earned through this chain:

1. Lily/Max discover each other.
2. They prove the phenomenon reacts predictably.
3. They learn basic timing/state rules.
4. They earn P1 through a controlled setup.
5. Success makes them overconfident.
6. They combine too many overlap-enhancing conditions.
7. Local instability follows.
8. The same rules learned earlier become the tools for stabilization.
9. They choose to give up stronger contact in order to protect themselves/others.
10. Their trust survives the consequence.

No outside expert may arrive and solve the climax for them.
