# 5. Scene Architecture

## Scene 1 — Paper Bag, Rent Receipt, Inspection Notice

**POV:** Lily  
**Location:** Aster House hallway → Unit 4B doorway  
**Approximate manuscript weight:** 450–650 words

### Scene Purpose

Re-establish Lily's ordinary routine after Arc 1 and introduce the inspection notice through a mundane moment.

### Entry State

Lily is coming home after an ordinary Paper Moon shift.

Useful mundane details may include:
- a paper bag or small groceries;
- work smell on her clothes;
- a rent receipt / utility reminder / laundry problem;
- realizing she still forgot something simple such as dish soap;
- thinking about sleep or the next shift rather than dimensional physics.

The room has been quiet enough that ordinary inconvenience has become important again.

### Trigger

Lily finds an official Aster House notice at or under her door.

The notice states that Inez will inspect the **east-side service area** because of:
- the earlier Unit 4B door/frame complaint;
- recurring cold/temperature concerns near 4B;
- old electrical/service routing;
- repair/insurance documentation.

The notice is ordinary in tone.

It must NOT contain supernatural language.

### Lily Reaction

Lily first reads it as a tenant problem:
- possible inconvenience;
- access interruption;
- landlord inspection;
- another thing to schedule around work.

Only after the ordinary reaction does she realize:
the east-side service area may be the sealed area adjacent to the wall involved in earlier anomalies.

Her curiosity rises, but she does not decide she has a right to enter.

### Comedy

Small, dry Lily humor only.

Possible source:
the notice contains a boring phrase like "service access review," and Lily thinks reality nearly broke through her apartment while the paperwork still manages to sound like a plumbing appointment.

Do not turn the scene into a joke sequence.

### Information Ceiling

Allowed:
- there will be an inspection;
- it concerns the east-side service area;
- it is connected to prior ordinary complaints.

Not allowed:
- what is physically inside the corridor;
- whether anything matches Larkspur;
- old marks;
- structural correspondence.

### Scene Turn

Instead of immediately contacting Max, Lily decides to ask Inez what the inspection actually involves.

This preserves:
**ordinary authority before dimensional curiosity.**

### Scene End

Lily sees Inez elsewhere in the building / hears her in the hall / decides to catch her before she leaves.

That directly causes Scene 2.

## Scene 2 — Mrs. Bell Has a Key for Everything

**POV:** Lily  
**Location:** Aster House hallway / stairs / building office area  
**Approximate manuscript weight:** 500–700 words

### Scene Purpose

Establish the legitimate access path and Inez's independent motivation.

### Inez Goal

Inez wants:
- the east-side service problem documented;
- repair costs kept under control;
- the insurer/repair contractor to stop asking for incomplete information;
- Aster House kept functional without unnecessary reconstruction.

She is not helping Lily investigate another reality.

### Dialogue Purpose

The conversation must answer:
1. Why is Inez inspecting the east-side service area?
2. Why may Lily accompany her?
3. What may Lily do during the inspection?
4. When will the inspection occur?
5. What does Inez need Lily to bring?

### Core Exchange

Lily asks whether the notice concerns the wall/service route near 4B.

Inez confirms the practical reason:
- older electrical/service routing;
- earlier frame/door complaint;
- temperature issue;
- repair documentation.

Lily asks whether she needs to be present.

Inez says Lily can accompany the inspection because:
- Lily made the original complaint;
- Inez needs Lily to point out the exact wall/area;
- Lily already has notes/photos from the earlier problem;
- having the tenant identify the complaint location saves Inez from guessing.

### Access Lock

Inez makes the boundary explicit:
- she unlocks the area;
- Lily goes in only with her;
- Lily does not receive a key;
- Lily is there to identify/document the complaint area;
- this is not permission to wander through closed service spaces later.

This must feel like normal landlord authority, not a story-rule lecture.

### Characterization

Inez:
- practical;
- financially aware;
- not easily impressed by Lily's oddly specific questions;
- willing to let Lily help when useful;
- retains control of her building.

Lily:
- does not lie more than necessary;
- does not dump cosmology;
- does not manipulate Inez into access;
- respects the restriction.

### Comedy

Possible source:
Lily asks one question too specifically—such as how old a wall section is—and Inez gives her a look that implies tenants normally ask about mold, not construction chronology.

The joke remains character-based.

### Important Continuity Choice

Use **Mrs. Bell** naturally in Lily-facing prose/dialogue if that matches existing manuscript habit, while the production file identifies her as Inez Bell.

### Scene Turn

Inez gives a **specific inspection appointment**.

Recommended scheduling form:
- next day or near-future;
- after/before Lily's shift in a way that creates a real scheduling constraint;
- exact clock time should be chosen in manuscript and then carried into continuity.

Inez tells Lily to bring:
- previous photos;
- the written repair/temperature notes;
- anything that shows where the earlier frame/wall complaint occurred.

### Scene End

Lily now has something concrete enough to tell Max:
not "I found a secret corridor," but
**"I have permission to attend an inspection."**

That directly causes Scene 3.

## Scene 3 — Opportunity Is Not Permission to Be Stupid

**POV:** Lily  
**Location:** Unit 4B — Correlation Patch A  
**Approximate manuscript weight:** 650–850 words

### Scene Purpose

Translate the maintenance appointment into a bounded research opportunity while demonstrating that CH-020's Roommate Agreement actually changes behavior.

### Communication Mode

Use **P0 only**.

Preferred:
- written exchange through Correlation Patch A;
- optionally a very brief bounded audio moment only if pacing genuinely needs it.

P1 is unnecessary and should not be used.

No communication follows Lily into the future corridor.

### Opening Beat

Lily tells Max:
- Inez is inspecting the east-side service area;
- Lily may accompany her;
- the reason is maintenance, not dimensional research;
- access is supervised and time-bounded.

Max immediately recognizes the possible relevance.

His first response may be enthusiastic enough for comedy, but he must not pressure Lily.

### Roommate Agreement Callback

Lily physically has, sees, or remembers the Roommate Agreement.

Use the rules as decision tools, especially:
1. home/work/family before experiments;
2. no test without both agreeing;
3. stop means stop;
9. clear question / expected result / stop condition / recovery plan;
10. either person may refuse.

The agreement should not be reprinted in full.

### Central Discussion

Lily and Max define what CH-023's first inspection is **not**.

Not:
- a test;
- a synchronization attempt;
- a P1 session;
- a hunt for a mystical object;
- permission to manipulate walls;
- permission to stay after Inez leaves.

It is:
- an observation opportunity;
- a chance to identify fixed structural features;
- a chance to take ordinary measurements/photos only if Inez allows them;
- a starting point for later comparison.

### Relationship Beat

R2 comfort should be visible through:
- Max treating Lily's work schedule as real, not an obstacle;
- Lily trusting Max enough to tell him before she knows whether the inspection will produce anything;
- banter around research rules;
- Max accepting a restriction without sulking.

Do not make the moment romantic.

### Comedy

Primary joke source:
Max tries to give the passive observation a dramatic technical name.

Lily reduces it to something boring such as:
**LOOK AT WALL. WRITE THINGS DOWN.**

Max objects to the lack of scientific dignity.

This is allowed because it reinforces their different voices and the low-risk method.

### Character Growth

Lily:
- explicitly refuses to miss/jeopardize work for the inspection unless the schedule legitimately allows it;
- refuses unauthorized access.

Max:
- does not suggest a stronger contact mode;
- accepts that he needs to understand his own safe Larkspur range before comparing structures.

This last point creates CH-022.

### Mystery / System State

No M-ID changes.

No F-ID changes.

No new system event.

The only new knowledge is logistical:
**there will be legitimate access to a potentially relevant Earth-side service area.**

### Final Beat

Inez sends / leaves / confirms the final inspection time and reminds Lily to bring the earlier notes/photos.

Lily gives Max the time.

Max responds that before Lily measures anything, he needs to know exactly how far he can safely survey on his side.

Possible final exchange shape:

- Lily: inspection time confirmed.
- Max: then he needs a safe boundary first.
- Lily: no improvising.
- Max: agrees.

The chapter closes on practical preparation, not danger.

### End-State Handoff

Direct setup for CH-022:
**Max must define "The Safe Distance."**
