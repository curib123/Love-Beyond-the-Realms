# CH-013 — One More Test

Max made it three steps farther than usual before the corridor folded.

That should have counted as progress.

It did not feel like progress when the wall doubled, his stomach dropped, and he found himself back beside Room 17 with one hand braced against the doorframe.

"Excellent," he muttered. "I have conquered almost two additional meters."

The corridor did not applaud.

Beyond the nearest opening, Rivenhall continued without him. A railcar passed between buildings. Someone shouted from the market level below. Ordinary movement. Ordinary distance.

Max stayed where he was until the pressure behind his eyes faded.

Then he went back inside.

The writing patch was already open.

**MIRROR FIXED.**

He smiled before he could stop himself.

**GOOD. LANDLORD STILL TERRIFYING?**

Lily's reply came quickly.

**MORE THAN DIMENSIONAL PHYSICS.**

That seemed fair.

---

They used the repaired mirror once.

Only once.

Lily's image appeared in the polished face of Max's compass for several seconds, stable enough that he could see her raise an eyebrow when he checked the angle twice.

"You're doing it again," she said through a brief audio window.

"Doing what?"

"Adjusting something that already works."

"It could work better."

"That sentence is how my wall got repaired."

Max stopped touching the compass.

Lily looked satisfied.

The image flickered out.

The silence afterward felt heavier than it should have.

Max stared at the compass for a long moment.

Then he wrote:

**I NEED TO TRY SOMETHING STRONGER.**

The response took longer this time.

**WHY?**

He had several answers prepared.

None of them survived the question.

---

Max sat down.

The next window opened faintly.

He wrote slowly enough that the words would fit.

**THE ACCIDENT AT LARKSPUR WASN'T RANDOM FOR ME.**

Lily answered:

**WHAT DOES THAT MEAN?**

Max looked toward the corridor.

He could still remember running.

A warning tone. People shouting. Light bending where it should not bend.

And Mina somewhere ahead of him.

He wrote:

**MY SISTER WAS THERE. MINA.**

The marks blurred at the edge.

A new window formed before Lily answered.

**I WAS TRYING TO REACH HER WHEN THE PLACE COLLAPSED.**

Then:

**I WOKE UP LIKE THIS.**

His pen stopped.

The last part was harder.

**I DON'T KNOW WHAT HAPPENED TO HER AFTER.**

For several seconds, nothing appeared.

Then Lily wrote:

**YOU NEVER TOLD ME.**

Max almost wrote a joke.

Instead:

**I KNOW.**

Another pause.

**I THOUGHT IF I COULD UNDERSTAND THIS FIRST, I'D HAVE SOMETHING USEFUL TO SAY.**

Lily's answer came slowly.

**THAT IS NOT THE SAME AS HAVING TO SOLVE IT ALONE.**

Max looked away.

Annoying sentence.

Accurate sentence.

Those were often the worst kind.

---

He waited until the next stable window before showing her the plan.

Not a crossing attempt.

Not contact.

A stronger spatial reading.

His compass already helped hold Max's side steady. Lily's brass apartment key behaved like an anchor in earlier observations, and the repaired mirror could provide a known P1 reference.

If they aligned all three with the same count, Max might be able to tell whether his phase-bound state had a direction.

Toward somewhere.

Toward someone.

Lily read the short version twice.

Then she wrote:

**DAYTIME.**

**AGREED.**

**NO WORK DAY FOR ME.**

**AGREED.**

**ONE ATTEMPT.**

Max hesitated.

Then wrote:

**AGREED.**

A final line appeared.

**IF EITHER OF US SAYS STOP, WE STOP.**

Max looked at it longer than necessary.

He wanted to argue for two attempts.

Maybe three.

Enough data to matter.

Instead he wrote:

**AGREED.**

The patch began to fade.

Before it closed, Lily added:

**THIS IS NOT YOU SAVING EVERYONE.**

Max leaned back.

He wished she were wrong.

That would have been easier.

The compass rested beside his hand.

One stronger test.

A controlled one.

A useful one.

Max told himself those were different things.
