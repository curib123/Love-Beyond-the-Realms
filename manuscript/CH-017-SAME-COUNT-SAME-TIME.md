# CH-017 — Same Count, Same Time

Max had spent enough time trying to make the connection stronger.

That was probably the problem.

He sat at the worktable with every old timing note spread in front of him and crossed out the plans that involved adding anything.

No stronger mirror alignment.

No tighter compass field.

No key.

No new variable.

The room had become wrong after they pushed too many things into agreement at once.

So this time, they would do less.

The writing patch opened.

**PLAN?**

Max wrote:

**MAKE EVERYTHING BORING.**

Lily's reply took forty-one seconds.

**FINALLY A FIELD YOU'RE QUALIFIED FOR.**

He smiled.

Good.

If she could still insult him, the situation was not completely lost.

---

They began by putting both rooms back into their known positions.

Lily moved the chair away from the correlation point.

Max returned his stool to its old mark.

Lily set the repaired mirror to its normal wall angle instead of the P1 angle.

Max lowered the compass flat on the table.

No active alignment.

No visual window.

Only writing.

Then they measured lag.

Max made one short mark.

Lily answered when it appeared.

Forty-seven seconds.

Again.

Thirty-two.

Again.

Eleven.

Max stared at the number.

The lowest delay they had seen since the geometry went wrong.

He wrote:

**NEXT WINDOW. SAME COUNT.**

Lily answered:

**WHAT CHANGES?**

Max kept it simple.

**NOTHING STRONGER. WE BOTH RETURN ONE THING TO NEUTRAL ON THREE.**

For Lily, the last part of the mirror angle.

For Max, the compass orientation.

Small.

Controlled.

Reversible.

The answer came.

**ONE TRY.**

He wrote:

**ONE.**

---

The next stable window came twelve minutes later.

Max watched the doorway.

Its false outline still sat slightly left of the real frame.

The writing patch brightened.

**READY.**

Max put one hand beside the compass.

He did not touch the calibration ring.

Lily's count appeared one number at a time.

**3**

Delay.

**2**

Shorter delay.

**1**

Max moved the compass to its neutral storage angle.

At the same moment, he watched the false doorway.

It jerked.

Not closed.

Closer.

The duplicate edge moved toward the physical frame by several inches.

Max held still.

The patch flickered.

Lily wrote:

**MINE MOVED TOO.**

A second copy of the message did not appear.

Max checked the dial.

Nine seconds.

Still delayed.

Better.

The wall beside him stopped doubling.

Not completely.

But enough that his eyes no longer had to decide which edge was real.

He let out a breath.

"All right."

The room did not answer.

For once, that felt encouraging.

---

They waited another ten minutes without touching anything.

No new adjustment.

No second test.

The doorway remained imperfect but stable.

Max wrote:

**IT RESPONDS TO COORDINATED NEUTRAL CHANGES.**

Lily answered:

**SO WE DON'T FIX IT BY PUSHING HARDER.**

He looked at the line.

**APPARENTLY NOT.**

A pause.

Then:

**THAT MUST BE DIFFICULT FOR YOU.**

Max put a hand over his heart.

She could not see the gesture.

That was probably why he survived it.

He looked at the compass.

The key lesson was not that the room needed more alignment.

It needed less.

Their strongest anchors had been held in agreement when the geometry broke.

If they wanted the room to return to baseline, the final move had to be the opposite.

Release them.

Together.

Not one side first.

Not one person trying to carry the risk.

Same count.

Same time.

Max wrote:

**FINAL STEP TOMORROW. WE RELEASE THE ACTIVE ANCHORS TOGETHER.**

Lily's reply arrived.

**ON THREE?**

Max smiled.

**ON THREE.**

The false doorway flickered once.

Then settled almost on top of the real frame.

Almost.

Not enough.

But for the first time since the stronger test, Max could see a path back to normal that did not require making anything stronger.

That felt like progress.
