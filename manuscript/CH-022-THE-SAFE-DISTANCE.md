# CH-022 — The Safe Distance

Max woke to the smell of cold tea.

That was his first mistake.

The second was discovering the cup beside his bed and deciding one swallow might still be worth the risk.

It was not.

He made a face and put it back down.

"Terrible."

Room 17 offered no sympathy.

Morning light reached the floor through the tall opening at the far end of the room, pale and clean compared to the amber lamps that would take over later. Somewhere below, someone rolled a cart across stone. A door slammed. Two voices argued briefly, then moved on.

Normal life.

Close enough to hear.

Max sat up and rubbed both hands over his face.

No headache.

No nausea.

No doubled edges around the wall.

Good.

He checked again anyway.

Still good.

Only then did he get up.

His shirt from yesterday hung over the back of a chair instead of the hook three steps away. One boot had somehow ended up under the table. His blanket was half on the floor.

A highly controlled research environment.

He found water, ate the last piece of flatbread from the wrapped packet on the shelf, and forced himself to finish both before touching the compass.

That part felt suspiciously responsible.

He blamed Lily.

By the time the writing patch appeared, he had a notebook open, measuring cord coiled beside it, pencil sharpened, and one very clear line at the top of the page.

**FIRST REAL SYMPTOM = RETURN.**

Max looked at it.

Then added:

**NO FIVE MORE SECONDS.**

The patch brightened.

**YOU AWAKE?**

He picked up the pen.

**OFFENSIVE QUESTION.**

A pause.

**THAT IS NOT A YES.**

Max glanced at the bed.

**MOSTLY.**

The answer came back quickly.

**MAX.**

He sighed.

"See? This is harassment."

He wrote:

**YES. I SLEPT.**

A beat.

**HOW MUCH?**

He stared at the page.

This was no longer science.

This was an audit.

**ENOUGH.**

Nothing.

He waited.

Still nothing.

Max narrowed his eyes.

"That is manipulative."

The page remained blank.

He gave up.

**ABOUT SEVEN HOURS.**

The reply came immediately.

**GOOD.**

Max pointed at the page.

"I knew you were there."

Another line appeared.

**ANY HEADACHE?**

**NO.**

**NAUSEA?**

**NO.**

**VISION?**

Max looked toward the doorway to the adjoining passage.

Everything had exactly one edge.

**NORMAL.**

Lily took longer with the next answer.

**THEN STOP WHEN THAT CHANGES. NOT WHEN YOU THINK YOU CAN HANDLE MORE.**

Max looked at the line at the top of his notebook.

**I WROTE IT DOWN.**

**THAT DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY MAKE ME TRUST YOU.**

He smiled.

**HURTFUL.**

**ACCURATE.**

Fair.

Max tapped the pen once against the table.

**YOUR INSPECTION STILL 10:30?**

**YES.**

**WORK AT 1?**

**YES.**

Good.

Ordinary schedule intact.

He liked that more than he would have admitted a month ago.

Lily added:

**AND YOU DON'T NEED TO PROVE ANYTHING BEFORE THEN.**

Max's smile faded a little.

He looked toward the service passage.

That sentence had found the exact place he was trying not to look at.

He wrote:

**I KNOW.**

A pause.

Then:

**I'M GOING TO FIND THE SAFE DISTANCE. THAT'S ALL.**

Lily answered:

**GOOD.**

No joke.

No lecture.

Just good.

Max closed the notebook.

That somehow made the promise feel more serious.

---

The first few steps outside Room 17 were easy.

Not comfortable.

Easy.

There was a difference.

The directly adjoining service passage was narrow enough that Max could touch both walls if he stretched his arms. He did not.

Old utility covers ran along one side. The stone had been patched in three different shades. A thin pipe disappeared into the ceiling at the first bend.

Nothing moved.

Nothing doubled.

No pressure.

Max marked the threshold distance in his notebook.

Then the first bend.

Then a fixed wall recess.

Simple points.

No interpretation.

He was not comparing anything to Lily's building yet.

He was only asking one question.

How far could he go and still trust what he saw?

The measuring cord made a soft scrape against the floor.

Max stopped.

Listened.

Nothing unusual.

Farther down, voices passed somewhere beyond the walls.

A laugh.

A cough.

Someone complaining about a delivery.

The sound lasted only a few seconds before fading deeper into Larkspur.

Max stood still until it was gone.

Then he moved again.

The passage opened slightly near the first structural junction.

Not much.

Just enough for a shallow inspection alcove built into the wall beside the turn.

Max had reached this area before.

Usually badly.

Usually while pretending symptoms were negotiable.

Today, he slowed before the line in the stone where the passage widened.

One step.

Fine.

Another.

Still fine.

He looked at the alcove.

Fixed recess.

Utility channel.

Old fastening points.

Useful later.

Not today.

Max took one more step.

Pressure touched the space behind his eyes.

Small.

Immediate.

He stopped.

The hard edge of the alcove doubled.

Not much.

A thin second line sat beside the first.

Max did not move.

His grip tightened around the pencil.

There.

First real symptom.

He looked down at the notebook.

The line he had written that morning was not here.

Room 17 was behind him.

Lily was farther away than that.

No writing patch.

No voice.

No one to say his name in that specific tone that meant he had already lost the argument.

The junction was right there.

One more step would give him a cleaner angle.

Maybe two.

He could measure the recess directly instead of estimating from the safe side.

Five seconds.

Less.

Max almost laughed.

Of course his brain had chosen that number.

He looked past the junction.

Nothing dramatic waited there.

No impossible door.

No glow.

Just more passage.

That made it worse.

Danger that looked ordinary was easier to bargain with.

The pressure sharpened slightly.

A faint wave of nausea followed.

Max closed his eyes.

"No."

The word sounded small in the passage.

Good enough.

He marked the spot without moving forward.

**FIRST SYMPTOM — JUNCTION.**

Then, under it:

**STOP POINT.**

He looked toward the alcove again.

His old habit immediately offered a better sentence.

*One step beyond for confirmation.*

Max pictured Lily reading that report later.

Not angry.

Worse.

Quiet.

Because if he lied about where the symptoms started, every later comparison they made would be built on bad information.

That was not useful.

That was just dangerous with neat handwriting.

Max turned around.

The pressure eased after several steps.

The doubled edge disappeared.

By the first bend, the nausea was gone.

He kept walking anyway.

No stopping to recheck.

No turning around for a cleaner number.

Room 17 came back into view.

Max crossed the threshold.

Then leaned both hands on the worktable.

"Very heroic," he muttered.

His pulse disagreed.

---

He did not write to Lily immediately.

That was new too.

Max sat down.

Drank water.

Waited.

The pressure behind his eyes faded completely within a few minutes.

He checked the wall.

One edge.

The table.

One edge.

His hand.

One hand.

Encouraging.

Only then did he record the recovery time.

The writing patch appeared while he was still finishing the note.

**BACK?**

Max looked at it.

Of course.

He wrote:

**BACK.**

The next line came fast.

**ARE YOU OKAY?**

Not *What did you find?*

Not *How far?*

Max read the question twice.

Then:

**YES.**

He added before she could ask:

**ACTUALLY YES.**

A pause.

**THAT SECOND LINE WAS NECESSARY.**

He smiled.

**I KNOW.**

Another pause.

**SYMPTOMS?**

Max looked at the notebook.

For half a second, the old instinct returned.

Make it sound cleaner.

Milder.

More controlled.

He ignored it.

**PRESSURE BEHIND EYES AT FIRST JUNCTION. SLIGHT DOUBLING. BRIEF NAUSEA.**

The reply took longer.

**AND YOU STOPPED THERE?**

Max tapped the pencil against the page.

**YES.**

Nothing.

Then:

**YOU DIDN'T GO PAST IT?**

He wrote:

**NO.**

Another pause.

Longer this time.

Max leaned back.

"What?"

The answer appeared.

**GOOD.**

He looked at that word.

Same as before.

Different now.

He wrote:

**I FEEL LIKE I DESERVE MORE CEREMONY.**

**YOU FOLLOWED YOUR OWN RULE.**

**EXACTLY. HISTORIC EVENT.**

**I'M TRYING NOT TO SCARE IT AWAY.**

Max laughed.

There she was.

He pulled the notebook closer.

**SO. CURRENT CONDITIONALLY RELIABLE OUTER SURVEY LIMIT—**

The writing appeared before he finished.

**SAFE DISTANCE.**

Max stared.

**THAT IS LESS PRECISE.**

**IT IS FOUR WORDS SHORTER.**

**PRECISION HAS A COST.**

**SO DO HEADACHES.**

He stopped.

Fair.

Max crossed out the longer phrase in his notes.

Not completely.

Just enough to show disagreement.

Then he wrote:

**SAFE DISTANCE: FIRST STRUCTURAL JUNCTION / INSPECTION ALCOVE.**

Lily answered:

**BETTER.**

He hated how satisfying that looked.

They worked through the rule once.

Start from Room 17.

Measure only fixed features inside the known safe range.

First real symptom means return.

Recover before comparing anything.

Then Lily added:

**AND PAST THE STOP POINT?**

Max looked at the blank part of the page beyond the junction mark.

He knew what the old version of him would have written.

*Unverified.*

*Possible later survey.*

*Needs better conditions.*

Instead, he drew a heavy line after the junction.

Beyond it, in block letters:

**NOT DATA**

He copied the same words into the writing patch.

Lily's answer took a few seconds.

**GOOD.**

Max sat back.

No grand discovery.

No answer about Aster House.

No explanation for why the passage behaved differently near the junction.

Just a line he would not cross and pretend meant something.

Tomorrow Lily would enter the east-side service area with Mrs. Bell.

She would have her own walls to measure.

Her own limits.

And when they compared notes later, at least one side of the comparison would not be built on Max pretending he was fine.

He looked at the words again.

**NOT DATA**

For once, stopping had given them something useful.
