# CH-021 — The Inspection Notice

By the time Lily reached the fourth floor, the paper bag in her left hand had started cutting into her fingers.

She shifted it to the other hand.

Bread.

Instant noodles.

Milk.

A bag of apples she had bought because buying fruit made her feel more organized than she actually was.

No dish soap.

Again.

Lily stopped halfway down the hall.

She stared at the grocery bag.

"Of course."

Paper Moon had been busy enough that afternoon to leave the smell of coffee in her hair and a faint ache between her shoulders. Someone had spilled iced chocolate on a stack of used travel books. A child had put three bookmarks into the tip jar. Ava had sent her six messages about a university application form and then disappeared before answering Lily's one useful question.

It had been a normal day.

Very normal.

Lily had discovered that normal days were exhausting in ways dimensional disasters were not.

Dimensional disasters did not usually require her to remember dish soap.

She reached Unit 4B and saw the paper pushed halfway under her door.

Her first thought was rent.

Her second was that rent was not due yet.

Her third was that anything official-looking outside Unit 4B had recently become suspicious by association.

Lily set the grocery bag down and picked up the notice.

ASTER HOUSE  
SERVICE ACCESS INSPECTION

East-side service area inspection scheduled for tomorrow morning.

Reason for inspection:

- follow-up on Unit 4B frame and alignment complaint;
- temperature irregularity near east wall;
- review of older electrical and service routing;
- repair and insurance documentation.

Tenants affected by access will be contacted directly.

— I. Bell

Lily read it twice.

Then once more.

At first, all she saw was inconvenience.

Tomorrow morning.

She had a Paper Moon shift at one.

If Mrs. Bell needed access to 4B, Lily would have to clean the desk.

Not because it was dirty.

Because three notebooks, two rulers, a roll of tape, a spatial diagram copied from another reality, and a handwritten document titled ROOMMATE AGREEMENT did not create the strongest impression of a tenant who had everything under control.

Then her eyes went back to one line.

**East-side service area.**

Lily looked at the wall beside her door.

Nothing happened.

No wrong light.

No cold.

No second hallway.

Just faded paint and the tiny mark where Mrs. Bell's repairman had checked the frame after everything had finally settled down.

The east side.

The sealed service area ran somewhere behind that part of the building.

Mrs. Bell had mentioned old service routes before. Pipes. Electrical lines. Parts of Aster House that had been changed so many times nobody seemed completely sure what belonged to which renovation.

Lily folded the notice once.

Reality had nearly opened the wrong hallway through her apartment.

Mrs. Bell had managed to describe the follow-up as **repair and insurance documentation**.

There was probably a lesson in that.

Mostly about paperwork surviving everything.

Lily picked up the grocery bag.

She could tell Max.

Not yet.

The Roommate Agreement existed partly because "interesting" had become a dangerous word.

First she needed to know what Mrs. Bell actually meant by inspection.

A heavy set of keys clinked from the stairwell.

Lily looked over.

Mrs. Bell appeared around the corner carrying a small metal toolbox in one hand and a folder under her arm.

Perfect timing.

Probably.

"Mrs. Bell?"

Mrs. Bell stopped.

Her eyes moved from Lily to the notice in Lily's hand.

"You found it."

"Hard to miss."

"I could make the paper smaller next time."

"Please don't."

Mrs. Bell gave her the kind of look that meant she had already spent too much of the day talking to contractors.

Lily held up the notice.

"The east-side service area. Is that behind this side of the building?"

Mrs. Bell shifted the folder under her arm.

"Partly."

"Partly?"

"Aster House has been renovated by people who apparently considered straight lines a personal insult."

That sounded more believable than it should have.

Mrs. Bell continued down the hall, and Lily walked with her.

"The electrician wants the older route checked before he signs off on anything," she said. "The insurer wants photographs. The repair company wants to know whether your door complaint came from the frame, the wall, or something farther inside."

Lily kept her face neutral.

"Something farther inside" meant something very different to her than it did to Mrs. Bell.

Probably.

"And the cold spot?"

Mrs. Bell glanced at her.

"You reported it."

"I know."

"Then yes. That too."

They reached the stair landing.

Mrs. Bell put the toolbox down for a moment and opened the folder.

Receipts.

A printed floor plan.

Two repair forms.

Nothing glowing.

Nothing impossible.

Lily was almost disappointed in herself for checking.

"How old is that section?" she asked.

Mrs. Bell slowly looked up.

"What?"

"The east wall. Or the service area behind it. Is it original to the building?"

The silence lasted just long enough.

"Most tenants ask if there is mold."

"I can ask that too."

"There probably isn't."

"Good."

Mrs. Bell narrowed her eyes slightly.

Lily tried to look like a person with a completely normal interest in construction history.

It was not one of her better skills.

Mrs. Bell closed the folder.

"Some of that side is old. Some was redone. Some was covered over before I owned the place. That's why I'm checking it instead of paying someone to guess."

That was fair.

"Do you need me here?"

"I need you to show me exactly where you noticed the frame problem and the temperature change."

Lily looked back toward 4B.

"So I can come with you?"

Mrs. Bell's expression changed immediately.

"With me."

"Right."

"Not by yourself."

"I wasn't asking for a key."

"You were about to."

"I was not."

Mrs. Bell picked up the toolbox.

"You had the face."

Lily had no idea what face meant.

Apparently Mrs. Bell did.

"The service door stays locked," Mrs. Bell said. "I open it. You come in with me. You show me the area connected to your complaint. If I need photos or measurements, you can help."

That was more permission than Lily had expected.

It was also very clearly not freedom.

"And after?"

"After, I lock it again."

Simple.

Reasonable.

Extremely inconvenient for secret cross-reality investigation.

Which was probably the point of locks.

"When?"

"Ten-thirty tomorrow morning."

Lily mentally checked her schedule.

"My shift starts at one."

"Then we'll be done before noon."

Good.

Mrs. Bell opened the folder again and pulled out a small note.

"Bring the pictures you took after the door problem. The ones with the tape on the floor."

Lily blinked.

"You remember those?"

"I remember anything that might save me from paying twice for the same repair."

Of course she did.

"And your temperature notes," Mrs. Bell added. "You wrote times down, didn't you?"

Lily had.

Some because Mrs. Bell had asked.

Some because Max had insisted timestamps mattered.

Most because after a while she had started recording anything Unit 4B did that seemed remotely strange.

"I have them."

"Bring them."

Mrs. Bell started down the stairs.

Lily looked at the notice again.

"Mrs. Bell?"

The landlord stopped two steps below.

"Can I take my own notes while we're in there?"

Mrs. Bell stared at her for a moment.

"About the repair?"

"Yes."

That answer was technically true.

Mrs. Bell sighed.

"Fine. Just don't touch anything I tell you not to touch."

"I won't."

"And if the electrician says move, you move."

"Understood."

Mrs. Bell nodded once and continued downstairs.

The keys at her side clinked with every step.

Lily watched until she disappeared.

Then she looked down at the notice.

Ten-thirty.

Supervised.

Time-limited.

No key.

No wandering.

No touching anything without permission.

For once, the rules had arrived before the bad idea.

That felt like progress.

---

Max's first response was exactly what Lily expected.

**YOU HAVE ACCESS TO THE SEALED SIDE?**

Lily sat at the desk with the maintenance notice beside the Roommate Agreement.

The writing patch was faint but stable.

She wrote:

**SUPERVISED ACCESS.**

Twenty seconds later:

**STILL ACCESS.**

**MRS. BELL HAS THE KEY.**

**TEMPORARILY ACCEPTABLE.**

Lily stared at that.

**YOU DON'T GET TO APPROVE MY LANDLORD'S KEY POLICY.**

A pause.

**I WAS SUPPORTING IT.**

**VERY GENEROUS.**

The reply came back:

**THANK YOU.**

Lily smiled despite herself.

Then she pushed the Roommate Agreement closer.

This part mattered.

She wrote:

**THIS IS NOT A TEST.**

Max answered faster.

**AGREED. OBSERVATION ONLY.**

That helped.

Lily continued.

**NO P1.**

**AGREED.**

**NO TRYING TO MAKE ANYTHING REACT.**

A longer pause.

**AGREED.**

Lily tapped the pen against the desk.

**NO "WHILE YOU'RE THERE, TRY THIS."**

This time the delay felt offended.

**I HAVE GROWN AS A PERSON.**

**YOU WROTE "NO FIVE MORE SECONDS" LESS THAN A WEEK AGO.**

**EVIDENCE OF GROWTH.**

She looked at Rule Nine.

No stronger test without a clear question, expected result, stop condition, and recovery plan.

Tomorrow did not need any of that because tomorrow was not an experiment.

That was the point.

She wrote:

**I LOOK. I WRITE THINGS DOWN. IF SHE ALLOWS PHOTOS OR MEASUREMENTS, I TAKE THEM. THAT'S IT.**

Max's answer appeared in two parts.

**PASSIVE CROSS-REALM STRUCTURAL**

The writing faded.

Returned.

**RECONNAISSANCE.**

Lily stared at the phrase.

Then wrote:

**LOOK AT WALL. WRITE THINGS DOWN.**

Nothing appeared for almost half a minute.

Then:

**YOUR VERSION LACKS SCIENTIFIC DIGNITY.**

**IT HAS FEWER WORDS.**

**TRAGIC.**

Lily leaned back.

The apartment was quiet around her.

The mirror was normal.

The key hanging near the door was warm.

The chair still had the cardboard under one leg because apparently some arguments could survive reality itself.

She looked down at the maintenance notice.

**TEN-THIRTY TOMORROW.**

Max replied:

**YOUR SHIFT?**

Lily paused.

That question mattered more than the dramatic name.

**ONE.**

**THEN YOU LEAVE WHEN THE INSPECTION ENDS. NO STAYING FOR EXTRA DATA.**

She raised an eyebrow.

**THAT SOUNDS LIKE MY RULE.**

**SHARED CULTURAL RULE.**

Lily almost crossed the sentence out on instinct.

Wrong medium.

She wrote:

**I'M NOT MISSING WORK FOR THIS.**

Max answered:

**GOOD.**

No joke attached.

Just good.

Lily looked at the word for a second, then back at the agreement.

Maybe that was the useful part of having rules.

Not that they stopped curiosity.

They stopped curiosity from deciding everything.

She wrote:

**IF THERE'S ANYTHING FIXED IN THERE, I CAN RECORD POSITION. RECESSES. PIPES. WALL BREAKS. WHATEVER SHE LETS ME SEE.**

Max took longer to answer.

**DON'T COMPARE IT TO MY SIDE YET.**

Lily frowned.

Then his next line appeared.

**I DON'T KNOW MY SAFE RANGE WELL ENOUGH.**

That was new.

Not the limitation.

The admission.

A few weeks ago, Max might have treated "not far enough" as a challenge.

Now he was treating it as missing information.

Lily wrote:

**SO WHAT DO YOU DO?**

**MAP ROOM 17 TO THE SERVICE PASSAGE.**

Another line.

**FIND THE POINT WHERE I STOP.**

Lily looked at Rule Three.

Stop means stop. Immediately.

She wrote:

**NO IMPROVISING.**

The reply took only a few seconds.

**AGREED.**

Then:

**NO FIVE MORE SECONDS.**

Lily rested the pen against the page.

Outside, someone closed a door farther down the hall.

Pipes clicked inside the wall.

A car passed on the street below.

Normal sounds.

Tomorrow, Mrs. Bell would unlock an old service area because an electrician needed photographs and an insurance company wanted paperwork.

Lily would go with her because she was the tenant who had made the complaint.

She would look.

She would write things down.

Nothing more.

For now.

She added one last line.

**BRINGING THE OLD NOTES.**

Max answered:

**I'LL HAVE MY BOUNDARY BEFORE THEN.**

Lily looked at the notice.

Ten-thirty tomorrow morning.

A real time.

A real key.

A real door Mrs. Bell controlled.

No wrong corridor.

No forcing anything open.

Just one old part of Aster House they had never properly seen.

Lily folded the inspection notice and placed it beneath the Roommate Agreement.

For once, the next question had arrived with permission.
