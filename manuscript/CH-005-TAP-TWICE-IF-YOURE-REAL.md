# CH-005 — Tap Twice If You're Real

Max arrived at the worktable ten minutes early.

This was unnecessary. The room had no clock appointment system, the strange writing did not care about manners, and Lily could not see him waiting.

He still arrived early.

He placed a clean sheet in the center of the table, set his spatial compass beside it, and checked the narrow section where the marks usually appeared. Then he checked it again because doing the same thing twice was apparently how people prepared for impossible conversations.

The lumen strip above him remained steady.

"Fine. Be dramatic."

Nothing answered.

Max sat.

When the light finally dipped, a gray line appeared almost immediately.

READY?

Lily's handwriting.

He smiled before he could stop himself.

READY.

Then he added:

TEST. NO GUESSING.

Her response arrived eight seconds later.

GOOD. YOU NEED IT.

Max stared at the line.

"Excellent start."

He wrote the rules as briefly as he could.

I TAP TWICE. YOU WRITE ANY NUMBER 1–9.

A pause.

THEN YOU TAP?

Max wrote:

YES. I ANSWER DIFFERENT NUMBER.

The light flickered. The window would not stay open long.

START?

YES.

Max lifted two fingers and tapped the center of the worktable twice.

Tap. Tap.

For five seconds, nothing happened.

Then a number appeared in Lily's handwriting.

7.

Max had been thinking three.

Good.

He wrote:

2.

The gray seven faded while his two remained.

Another line appeared.

NOW ME.

A moment later, something knocked against the room.

Not the table exactly.

The sound seemed to come from inside the wood.

Tap. Tap.

Max went very still.

He had expected the mark exchange.

He had not expected that.

He wrote the first number that came into his head.

5.

Lily answered:

I PICKED 4.

Max frowned.

Then another line appeared.

YOU FAILED YOUR OWN TEST.

He laughed.

"That's not how this works."

The writing window closed.

For once, Max did not mind.

The sound of those two taps remained in his head.

---

The next window took almost an hour.

Max used the time badly.

He tried the corridor again, made it three steps farther than usual, and came back with enough pressure behind his eyes to make the walls pulse. Then he drank water, sat on the floor for several minutes, and told himself that testing two impossible things on the same day was efficient.

It was not.

When the worktable dimmed again, he returned to the chair.

Lily had already written:

SECOND TEST.

Max replied:

YOU'RE ENJOYING THIS.

YES.

That answer arrived too quickly.

He wrote:

WRITE A WORD. ANYTHING. DON'T SHOW ME YET.

A few seconds later:

DONE.

Max looked away from the paper, chose his own word, and wrote:

BRIDGE.

Lily's hidden word appeared beneath it.

KETTLE.

Max blinked.

He wrote:

WHY KETTLE?

Her response:

MINE IS LOUD.

That was an absurdly ordinary answer.

It helped more than it should have.

Max rested his hand beside the page.

A living person would have a kettle.

A fabricated message could also mention a kettle.

He knew that.

Still, the growing collection of useless details felt harder to dismiss than any single impressive answer.

Different weather. Different city. Different room. Bad kettle.

A life made of details.

The light above him dimmed further.

Max's compass gave a soft metallic click.

He looked at it.

The needle trembled.

Then a voice said, very faintly:

"Max?"

He stopped breathing.

The sound was thin, almost buried beneath static.

A girl's voice.

"Can you hear me?"

Max leaned toward the table.

"Yes."

The word came out too loud.

Static cracked across the room.

He heard a sharp breath from the other side.

"Oh."

Max almost laughed.

"Strong reaction."

"What did—"

Her voice clipped out.

"—say?"

"I said yes."

A pulse of pressure ran through the table.

The lights stretched.

Max heard Lily again, clearer for one second.

"You're actually—"

The sound vanished.

Silence returned so completely it felt physical.

Max stared at the worktable.

The whole exchange could not have lasted more than ten seconds.

Maybe less.

But there had been a voice.

A breath.

A reaction he had not predicted.

Not writing.

Not a delayed mark.

Someone had heard him and answered.

---

The next written window was weak.

Only part of the page responded.

Max wrote:

YOU HEARD ME?

Gray letters appeared slowly.

YES.

He hesitated, then wrote:

YOU'RE REAL.

The reply came after a longer delay.

SO ARE YOU.

Then another word appeared beneath it.

PROBABLY.

Max laughed hard enough that it hurt his head.

"Of course."

The gray letters faded.

He did not write anything else.

For the first time since Room 17 had begun misbehaving, Max was not trying to prove that something impossible was happening.

That part was over.

There was someone on the other side.

A person named Lily, in a place called Bellweather, with too much rain and a loud kettle.

He still had no idea where Bellweather was.

He still could not see her.

That had become a new problem.

A much more interesting one.
