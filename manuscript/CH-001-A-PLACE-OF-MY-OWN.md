# CH-001 — A Place of My Own

The elevator made a noise halfway to the fourth floor that did not inspire confidence.

Lily looked up at the metal ceiling as it groaned again. "That normal?"

Mrs. Bell, standing beside her with one hand on a box of books, did not even look concerned. "It's been doing that since before you were born."

Lily waited.

Mrs. Bell added, "Probably."

That was less comforting.

The elevator shuddered, then continued upward. Lily tightened her grip on the box in her arms. The word **KITCHEN** was written across the side in thick black marker. It contained two mugs, three novels, a desk lamp, and one shoe. Packing had stopped being organized around two in the morning.

The elevator reached the fourth floor with a soft thump.

"See?" Mrs. Bell said. "Perfectly fine."

Lily stepped out first. "Your standards are generous."

Mrs. Bell gave her a sideways look, then very slowly smiled.

Lily decided she liked her.

The fourth-floor hallway was narrow and quiet. A long window at the far end let in the gray afternoon light. Rain tapped against the glass. Somewhere outside, a tram bell rang twice before fading into the city noise.

Aster House was old. Not charming-old in the way rental listings liked to claim. Actual old. The floorboards had small scratches. The walls were too thick. The brass number plates on the doors had been polished so many times that some of the edges had gone soft.

But the rent was manageable. The tram stop was close. Paper Moon was only a short ride away.

And, most importantly, Unit 4B was hers.

Mrs. Bell stopped in front of the door. "Lock sticks when it's cold. Lift the handle a little when you turn the key."

"I remember."

"And the kitchen window doesn't like being forced."

"I remember that too."

"Trash goes out Tuesday and Friday."

Lily nodded.

Mrs. Bell looked at the box in Lily's arms. "Want me to carry that inside?"

"No, I've got it."

It came out too quickly. Mrs. Bell's eyes moved from Lily to the box, then back again.

Lily adjusted her grip. "I've got it," she repeated, this time less like she was defending a court case.

"All right."

Mrs. Bell passed over the box she had been holding instead.

Lily stared at it. "You said—"

"I said I'd carry one box to the door. I did."

Before Lily could argue, Mrs. Bell walked toward the elevator.

"If the plumbing makes a knocking sound, ignore it unless water appears somewhere water should not be."

"That's a very specific rule."

"Old building."

The elevator doors opened with another unhappy groan, and Mrs. Bell stepped inside.

Lily looked at Unit 4B, then at the two boxes now sitting around her feet, then at the elevator.

"Thanks," she called.

Mrs. Bell raised one hand before the doors closed.

Lily turned back to the apartment. The brass key slid into the lock.

It did stick.

She lifted the handle like Mrs. Bell had shown her and tried again. The lock clicked.

Lily stood still for half a second.

There it was. A small sound. Nothing special.

But it was her lock. Her key. Her door.

She pushed it open.

The apartment smelled faintly of dust, old wood, and whatever cleaning product Mrs. Bell used before new tenants moved in. The living room was smaller without furniture. The tall window faced the courtyard. A built-in cabinet sat against one wall beside an old mirror with a dark wooden frame.

The kitchen was compact enough that Lily could probably reach half of it without moving her feet. The bedroom door stood open.

She stepped inside and set the box down. The door closed behind her, and the apartment went quiet.

For the first time since she'd signed the lease, no one was standing beside her asking whether she was sure.

Lily looked around. "Okay," she said softly.

The word disappeared into the room.

She smiled. Not much. Just enough.

This could work.

---

Two hours later, Lily had learned three important things about independent living.

First, boxes multiplied when no one was watching. Second, nothing fit where she had imagined it would. Third, a small flat-packed shelf could become a personal enemy.

She sat on the floor with the instruction sheet spread across one knee. The shelf leaned against the wall in front of her, one side higher than the other.

Lily frowned at it. "No."

The shelf remained uneven.

She checked the screws again. They were correct.

Probably.

The smiling cartoon person in the corner of the instruction sheet appeared far too pleased with himself.

Her phone buzzed on top of a box.

**Aunt Nora: Need help?**

Lily read the message, looked at the shelf, then back at the message. Her thumb hovered over the screen.

She could call. Nora would come. She would probably bring food, a screwdriver set, two towels Lily had forgotten, and enough concern to fill the rest of the apartment.

Lily locked her phone and put it face down.

"I have books."

Ten minutes later, the shelf was being held level by a stack of old paperbacks while Lily tightened the last screw. When she carefully removed the books, it stayed upright.

Not straight.

But upright.

Lily leaned back on her hands and examined it. One corner sat slightly higher than the others.

She could live with that.

Probably.

"Modern design."

She got up before the shelf could hear her and change its mind.

The apartment slowly started looking less empty. She made the bed, put her work shirts in the bedroom cabinet, unpacked the kettle, and found the missing second shoe inside the same box.

"Of course."

She plugged in her phone charger beside the bed. Two mugs went into the kitchen. Three novels went onto the crooked shelf. The desk lamp went on the small table by the window.

By early evening, only the important things had been unpacked.

Which meant there were still nine boxes left.

Lily chose not to count them again.

She opened the built-in cabinet in the living room and placed a few cleaning supplies on the lower shelf. The door dragged slightly against the frame when she shut it. She pushed until it clicked into place.

Old building.

That phrase already explained a surprising amount.

Her phone buzzed again, then again. Lily ignored it while she folded an empty box.

It started ringing.

She looked at the screen.

**Aunt Nora**

Lily closed her eyes for one second, then answered. "Hi."

"You're alive."

"So far."

"Lily."

"I'm joking."

"I know. Mostly."

Lily carried the phone into the kitchen and opened one of the upper cabinets. Empty. Good. At least no forgotten jar from 2009 had been waiting for her.

"Did you get everything inside?" Nora asked.

"Yes."

"Did Mrs. Bell show you the lock?"

"Yes."

"The window?"

"Yes."

"The—"

"Yes."

There was a pause.

"I hadn't asked the next thing yet."

"I was being efficient."

"You were being difficult."

"That too."

Nora sighed, but Lily could hear the small smile behind it.

That helped. A little.

"Did you eat?"

Lily looked at the packet of crackers on the counter. "Yes."

"What did you eat?"

"Food."

"Lily."

"Crackers."

"Crackers are not dinner."

"There were six of them."

"That somehow makes it worse."

Lily opened the refrigerator. A bottle of water sat alone on the middle shelf.

It looked lonely.

She closed the door. "I'll order something."

"Please do."

"I was going to."

"You absolutely were not."

Lily leaned against the counter. Nora knew her too well.

That had once felt easy. Now sometimes it made Lily want to move three cities away just to prove there were still things no one could predict about her.

She looked through the living room toward the tall window. Rain had blurred the courtyard outside. The room was still mostly boxes and bare walls, but there was a lamp now.

Her lamp. Her crooked shelf. Her ridiculous six-cracker dinner.

It wasn't much.

It was hers.

"Do you want me to come by?" Nora asked.

Lily's fingers tightened slightly around the phone. "No."

The answer came too fast.

Nora went quiet.

Lily stared at the floor. She could already hear what she had sounded like.

Not independent.

Defensive.

There was a difference, even if she hated admitting it.

"I'm okay," Lily said, softer. "Really."

"I know."

That somehow made her chest tighten more than if Nora had argued.

Lily cleared her throat. "I just want tonight to be mine."

Another pause.

Then Nora said, "All right."

No guilt. No lecture.

Just that.

Lily looked toward the living room again.

The built-in cabinet was open.

She frowned. Only a few centimeters.

Still.

She was sure she had closed it.

Probably.

"Lily?"

"Yeah."

"You disappeared."

"I'm looking at the cabinet."

"Is something wrong with it?"

"No. The door opened."

"Old hinge?"

"Probably."

"Put something under the front leg if the floor's uneven."

"See? This is why I keep you around."

"I'm honored."

Lily crossed the room and pushed the cabinet door shut again. It clicked.

Definitely closed this time.

She gave it a small test pull.

Still closed.

There.

Problem solved.

"Text me after your shift tomorrow," Nora said.

"I will."

"Not at midnight."

"I get off before midnight."

"You know what I mean."

Lily smiled despite herself. "Fine."

"And order actual food."

"Yes, Aunt Nora."

"I love you."

The words landed gently.

Lily looked at the rain on the window. For one second, she thought of another kitchen. Another voice. A different kind of home.

The thought hurt, but not sharply.

Not tonight.

"Love you too," she said.

They ended the call.

Lily stood in the middle of the apartment for a moment, then looked at the cabinet.

Still closed.

Good.

---

By nine, the rain had settled into a steady sound against the windows.

Lily had eaten noodles from a paper container, taken a shower, and decided the rest of the boxes could remain society's problem until morning. She laid out her Paper Moon shirt for the next day, plugged in her phone, and checked the front door.

Locked.

She lifted the handle and tested it once.

Still locked.

Good.

The apartment felt different at night. Not frightening. Just quieter. The kind of quiet that made every normal sound stand out.

Water moving somewhere inside the pipes. A car passing outside. The refrigerator starting up. Floorboards settling.

Old building.

Lily turned off the kitchen light. The living room lamp was still on.

She walked toward it, already thinking about bed.

Then she stopped.

There was someone in the living room.

A young man stood near the far side of the room.

Lily's hand froze beside the light switch.

For one stupid second, her brain tried to fit him into something normal.

Mrs. Bell.

Wrong.

A neighbor.

Wrong.

Someone from the hallway.

The front door was locked.

He looked real. Not transparent. Not shadowy. Not like anything that belonged in a horror movie.

Just a boy—around her age, maybe a little older—standing in the middle of the apartment Lily had spent all day making hers.

He was looking away from her.

Lily did not move.

The locked door was behind her. The windows were closed. No one had come in.

And yet she was no longer alone.
