# CH-014 — The Key Goes Cold

Lily checked the time twice.

Daylight.

No work shift.

Mirror repaired.

Chair back where it belonged.

One attempt.

She had written the rules on paper even though she already knew them.

Max's reply appeared beside the list.

**YOU FORGOT ONE.**

Lily frowned.

**WHAT?**

**NO HEROICS.**

She stared at the line.

Then wrote:

**THAT RULE IS FOR YOU.**

The answer came back quickly.

**RUDE.**

**ACCURATE.**

Lily put the pen down.

At least they were starting honestly.

---

The brass key lay in the center of the desk patch.

It looked completely ordinary.

That made Lily suspicious.

The repaired mirror faced the desk at the same angle that had worked before. On Max's side, his compass would be aligned to the corresponding point.

They opened a narrow P1 window first.

Room 17 appeared in Lily's mirror.

Max stood beside his worktable.

He looked more serious than usual.

"Still one attempt," Lily said when the audio opened.

"One."

"If I say stop—"

"We stop."

"If you say stop—"

"We stop."

"Good."

Max lifted the compass.

Lily placed two fingers lightly against the brass key.

They counted together.

"Three."

"Two."

"One."

The reflected room sharpened.

Too much.

Lily saw Max's face clearly. The worktable behind him snapped into focus. Even the small marks beside his compass looked sharper than they had during any earlier window.

Max looked down at the instrument.

"Hold."

Lily did not move.

For two seconds, everything stayed steady.

Then the key went cold.

Not cool.

Cold enough that Lily's fingers jerked.

She almost pulled away.

"Max."

"I see it."

"You see what?"

"The reading changed."

The key's metal seemed to drain the warmth from her skin.

Lily let go.

The cold remained.

"Stop."

Max lowered the compass immediately.

"Stopped."

The mirror should have returned to normal.

It did not.

---

Room 17 vanished from the glass, but Unit 4B did not come back correctly.

Lily stared.

Her reflected doorway sat several inches farther left than the real doorway behind her.

She turned.

Real door.

Normal.

She looked back at the mirror.

Wrong door.

The floor line in the reflection also bent at an angle that did not exist in the room.

Her stomach tightened.

"Max?"

The audio cracked.

"I'm here."

"Something didn't reset."

"Same here."

Lily looked at the desk.

The key had begun frosting at the edges.

A thin pale layer.

She did not touch it.

"How bad?"

Max answered after a pause.

"My wall is in two places."

Lily swallowed.

That was worse than she had hoped.

"Don't touch anything."

"I wasn't planning to."

The audio cut out.

For one second, the mirror showed Lily's normal room.

Then the wrong doorway returned.

She felt nausea rise sharply and grabbed the desk edge.

The key gave a tiny metallic click against the wood.

Not movement.

Cooling.

Still wrong.

---

The writing patch flickered on.

**STATUS?**

Lily wrote carefully.

**MIRROR GEOMETRY WRONG. KEY STILL COLD.**

Max answered:

**ROOM 17 ALSO OFFSET.**

The letters arrived, vanished, then reappeared half a line lower.

Lily stopped writing.

That had not happened before.

She waited.

The key slowly lost its frost, but the mirror did not fully settle.

The reflected doorway shifted closer to its real position.

Not enough.

Lily wrote:

**WE ARE DONE. NO SECOND TRY.**

Max's answer came immediately.

**AGREED.**

No argument.

No joke.

That frightened her more than one would have.

The room stayed quiet.

Five minutes passed.

Then ten.

The key finally warmed enough that Lily could touch it without pain.

The mirror still showed a slight mismatch at the edge of the doorway.

Less than before.

Still there.

Lily sat down.

This had been one test.

One controlled test.

They had followed the rules.

And stopping the test had not stopped the result.

The writing patch opened one last time.

**LILY?**

She wrote:

**HERE.**

A pause.

**I'M SORRY.**

Lily looked at the words.

She wanted to tell him it was fine.

It was not.

So she wrote:

**WE FIX IT FIRST. THEN WE TALK.**

The answer came back.

**AGREED.**

Lily looked at the brass key.

It was warm again.

The room was not.
