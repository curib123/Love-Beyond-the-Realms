# CH-002 — Someone Else Is Using My Room

Lily slept badly.

Not dramatically badly. She did not wake screaming or discover muddy footprints beside the bed or hear anyone whispering her name through the wall.

She simply woke four times, checked the living-room window twice, and spent the last hour before sunrise staring at the ceiling while her brain replayed four seconds of a city that had not been Bellweather.

By seven-thirty, she had decided on a reasonable explanation.

There had to be one.

The problem was that she had not found it yet.

Lily stood in front of the tall window with a mug of coffee in one hand and the curtain pulled aside with the other. Morning rain had turned the glass gray. Across the street, the same brick building sat where it had always been. The pharmacy sign was off. Tram wires crossed the road at the correct angle.

No elevated rail.

No amber lights.

No impossible city.

She checked the latch again.

Locked.

She turned the desk lamp on and off three times, then immediately felt ridiculous.

"Good," she told herself. "Excellent scientific method."

Nothing happened.

She moved left and right, changed the curtain angle, held her phone screen against the glass, and tried to reproduce any reflection that could have looked like the structure she remembered.

Nothing came close.

That was irritating.

A bad dream would have been easier. A reflection would have been better. Even a migraine would have been familiar enough to complain about properly.

Instead, Lily had a perfectly normal window and a memory she could not fit anywhere.

She opened the notes app on her phone.

10:15 PM. Window changed for maybe 3–4 seconds. Amber lights. Elevated structure. Lamp dimmed at same time.

She stared at the words.

Then added:

Possibly tired.

That felt responsible.

She considered adding Possibly losing mind, but decided responsible had limits.

---

The rest of the morning tried very hard to become normal.

Lily showered, unpacked two more boxes, found the missing half of her bedsheets in a suitcase she had already checked, and discovered that the radiator made a clicking sound every few minutes even though it was not cold enough to be on.

Mrs. Bell had mentioned pipes.

Old building.

Normal.

At eleven, Lily sat at the small desk against the east wall with her laptop open and a blank sheet of paper beside it. She was trying to list everything she still needed for the apartment.

Trash bags.

Dish soap.

Extension cord.

A chair that did not require folded cardboard to survive.

She had just written detergent when something moved at the edge of the page.

Lily stopped.

There was a dark line near the top-left corner.

She stared at it.

It had not been there a second ago.

The line extended.

Not fast. Not like a pen moving.

More like ink becoming visible through paper.

A short vertical mark appeared, then another beside it. A thin horizontal line connected them. More marks followed, neat and narrow, forming what looked almost like part of a measurement grid.

Lily did not touch the page.

Her first thought was printer ink.

The printer was still in a box.

Her second thought was that she had written it without remembering.

That was worse.

She lifted both hands away from the desk and watched.

One final mark appeared.

Then nothing.

Lily stayed still for several seconds.

The marks were dark gray, not quite black. Too straight to be accidental pen scratches. They looked deliberate, but they did not form words she recognized.

She glanced at the apartment door.

Locked.

The chain was on.

The room was empty.

Lily looked back at the page.

"Okay."

The marks did not respond.

She leaned closer without touching them. The paper was definitely hers. Cheap white notebook paper. Slight bend in the bottom corner. Small coffee stain from ten minutes ago.

Only the writing was wrong.

She picked up her phone and took a picture.

By the time the camera focused, the marks had already started to fade.

Lily frowned.

She took three more pictures.

The last one captured almost nothing.

Within a minute, the page was blank again except for her shopping list.

Lily sat back.

The wrong city had lasted four seconds.

The wrong writing had lasted less than two minutes.

Neither had left anything useful behind.

"Very considerate."

Her voice sounded too loud in the room.

She did not like that either.

---

Lily spent the next twenty minutes doing the sort of things a person did when she absolutely did not want to admit that invisible writing had appeared on her desk.

She checked the underside of the desk.

Nothing.

She checked the wall.

Paint.

She looked at the cabinet beside the desk, opened it, closed it, and felt mildly betrayed when it behaved normally.

She moved the sheet of paper to the kitchen counter.

Nothing happened.

She returned it to the desk.

Still nothing.

Then, while she was standing beside the chair, the desk lamp dimmed.

Lily froze.

Not much.

Just enough.

The same kind of dimming as last night.

Her eyes dropped to the paper.

A dark speck appeared near the center.

Then another.

Lily moved before she could talk herself out of it.

She sat down, grabbed a pen, and wrote in large block letters:

WHO ARE YOU?

She stopped.

The question looked dramatic.

She considered crossing it out and replacing it with something more reasonable.

Like:

PLEASE EXPLAIN THE UNAUTHORIZED PAPER ACTIVITY.

That was worse.

She left the first version.

Nothing happened.

Lily waited.

Ten seconds.

Twenty.

The desk lamp flickered once.

The page remained unchanged.

She checked her phone clock.

Forty seconds.

"Of course."

She rested her elbows on the desk, then remembered the strange marks and immediately lifted them again.

One minute.

The lamp brightened.

Lily exhaled.

The window had been four seconds. The marks had appeared once. Maybe whatever had happened was already over.

She picked up the paper.

A faint gray stroke appeared beneath her question.

Lily stopped moving.

The stroke darkened.

Then another appeared.

Letters formed one by one.

Not her handwriting.

Narrower.

Sharper.

WHO

Lily's fingers tightened around the edge of the desk.

The next word came slower.

ARE

Then:

YOU?

Lily stared.

For a moment, neither she nor the room did anything.

Then a second line began.

I WAS ABOUT TO ASK YOU THAT.

Lily read it twice.

The handwriting was compact and slightly slanted. Whoever—or whatever—had written it used less space than she did.

The sentence was also annoying.

That somehow made it worse.

Lily looked around the apartment.

Still empty.

She looked under the desk even though she knew that made no sense.

Nothing.

Back to the page.

The first letters were already fading.

"Wait."

She grabbed her phone.

The camera opened too slowly.

By the time she took the picture, the final words were pale enough that the image would probably look like bad pencil smudges.

Lily lowered the phone.

The message disappeared.

Her own question remained.

That was new.

She touched the paper carefully.

Dry.

Normal.

No hidden ink.

No heat.

No second sheet underneath.

Just ordinary paper on an ordinary desk in an apartment that had stopped behaving like one.

Lily sat very still.

Last night, the room had shown her somewhere else.

Today, something from somewhere else had written back.

She had no idea what it was.

She had no idea if it was a person.

But it had understood the question.

And it had answered.

That changed the problem completely.
