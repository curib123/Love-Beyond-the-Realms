# CH-010 — I Can See You

Max had one advantage over yesterday.

Yesterday, he had been trying to make the reflection stronger.

Today, he was trying to make it arrive at the right time.

That distinction made him feel smarter than he deserved.

The compass sat on its stand beside the worktable, its polished face angled toward the point where Lily's mirror sometimes appeared. Max had copied the previous timing results into the margin of his notebook.

Two seconds.

Five.

Eight.

Unhelpful, except for one thing.

The delay changed.

So perhaps the receiver had to change with it.

The writing patch opened.

Lily's first message appeared.

READY?

Max wrote:

AS MUCH AS POSSIBLE.

THAT IS NOT REASSURING.

IT WASN'T FOR YOU.

A pause.

RUDE.

Max smiled.

"Good. Normal so far."

---

They started with the room.

Lily angled her mirror until Max's polished plate shimmered and replaced part of Room 17 with the reflected corner of Unit 4B.

Desk.

Curtain.

Warm lamp.

Empty space where Lily should have been.

Max wrote:

ROOM STABLE.

I'M IN POSITION.

He checked the compass dial.

"Raise your hand on three."

The audio channel did not open, so he wrote the count instead.

3

2

1

The familiar blur appeared several seconds late.

This time Max did not chase it.

He watched the timing.

Four-point-something seconds.

He adjusted the compass face by a fraction and reset the dial.

Again.

The second attempt produced a narrower blur, then split it into two overlapping shadows.

Max's eyes watered.

Lily's writing appeared.

BETTER?

Max considered lying.

WORSE IN A MORE INTERESTING WAY.

THAT IS A VERY MAX ANSWER.

He could hear her saying it even though the audio channel was closed.

That was probably a problem of a different kind.

---

They waited for Max's eye pressure to settle before trying again.

The third window opened weakly.

Max almost stopped there.

Then the reflected room sharpened.

He checked the dial.

"All right."

He adjusted for the delay they had just measured instead of the average from earlier trials.

The blur appeared.

Max turned the compass face a few degrees.

The blur narrowed.

For one breath, nothing happened.

Then the missing part of the reflection filled in.

A person stood in Lily's mirror.

Max forgot the dial.

She was younger-looking than the voice in his head had made her, though not younger than the age he had vaguely guessed. Her hair was tied back badly enough that several loose strands had escaped around her face. She wore an ordinary dark shirt with one sleeve pushed slightly higher than the other.

Mostly, she looked annoyed.

That felt correct.

Lily raised one hand.

This time Max saw it happen.

Not a blur after the fact.

Her actual hand.

Her actual face shifted as she watched a mirror that still showed her nothing from his side.

Max stared.

"Lily."

The audio channel crackled open just enough for her voice to reach him.

"Did it work?"

Max opened his mouth.

The image shook.

For a second Lily doubled, one version half a step behind the other.

Then both vanished.

Room 17 returned to the polished metal.

Max sat very still.

The whole clear image had lasted perhaps five seconds.

It had been enough.

---

The writing patch opened before he had recovered a useful expression.

DID IT WORK?

Max looked at the question.

He wrote:

YES.

The answer came quickly.

YES WHAT?

He laughed.

I SAW YOU.

No response for several seconds.

Then:

OH.

Max waited.

Another line appeared.

WAS I A SMUDGE?

NOT THIS TIME.

A pause.

GOOD.

Max rubbed the ache above his eye.

He could have described her. He could have said what he had expected and what had been wrong.

Instead he wrote:

YOU LOOKED ANNOYED.

The reply arrived almost immediately.

I WAS LOOKING AT YOU.

Max laughed hard enough that the headache objected.

He wrote:

FAIR.

The window started fading.

Lily added one last line.

MY TURN NEXT.

Max looked at the compass.

She had no image of him yet.

The connection had not become fair just because his side had worked first.

He answered:

SEPARATE CALIBRATION.

AGREED.

The page cleared.

Max leaned back and closed his eyes.

For days, Lily had been handwriting, timing, a clipped voice, bad weather, and arguments about furniture.

Now she had a face.

It did not make her more real.

They had already settled that.

It only made the distance harder to ignore.
